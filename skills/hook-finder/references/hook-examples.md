# Hook case studies

last_checked: 2026-11-14. Numbers are as reported by the cited sources; many are self-reported.

| Game | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| I Just Want to be Single!! (visual novel) | A TikTok of the **main menu** got ~571K views; wishlists went from ~3,000 to ~6,000. It came after a week of near-zero results when the dev almost quit. | Something unusual can hook even without gameplay; persistence matters. |
| Sledding Game (solo dev) | Funny clips posted throughout development; ~15.8K wishlists on page-open day, ~80K in week one; 100K+ copies in 5 days of Early Access (Apr 2026). | Physics comedy + posting during development builds an audience before launch. |
| DREADMOOR | Reached 200K wishlists; posts by IndieGameJoe brought +25K wishlists in a week with 2.5M views in 24h. | A clear "Known game, but different" pitch spreads; atmosphere sells, not just action. |
| YAPYAP | One announcement TikTok hit 1.5M views; 200K+ wishlists before Next Fest Oct 2025. | Survivor case; the studio had a prior game and audience. Don't expect this by default. |
| Tiệm Phở của Anh Hai (Vietnam) | Free on itch.io (Oct 2025), near-zero plays for 5 days, then ~650K downloads by early Nov and ~3M by May 2026 after big YouTubers and a ~14M-view TikTok; Vietnamese brands joined the trend. | Hyper-specific local details (food, memes, places) create recognition and memes; a free build lets streamers play instantly. |
| fly.pieter.com | Built live on X with Cursor + Three.js; self-reported big early revenue from in-game ads, later reported near $0/month. | Building in public works when you already have an audience; spikes are not businesses. |

## Caution

- Claims like "35% of wishlists come from short video" circulate without transparent methods — don't repeat them as fact.
- For every viral example there are many devs who posted consistently with little result (presskit.gg). Set expectations accordingly.
